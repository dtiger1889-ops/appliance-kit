# Briefing style

Write for someone tired, holding a phone, who has never opened the manual.

- **Plain words.** "The mesh screen on the tub floor", not "the fine filter assembly". Give the part number once, in brackets, for searching.
- **Answer first.** The summary says what it is, how old, whether it's recalled, and the one thing to know.
- **Short steps,** one action each, in the order you'd do them. Say how often ("monthly").
- **Safety in the step it applies to** (wait for the heater to cool, unplug first), not in a block of warnings.
- **Sources on every step.** "(Manual p. 14)" or the site's name.
- **Honest gaps.** If the manual doesn't cover something, say so: "This manual has no filter section." No invented steps.
- **No repair language.** No "disassemble", no part replacement steps. "Call for service" with the reason.
- **Warranty runs from the purchase date, not the build date.** Give the warranty length the manual states; say it has ended only if the person told you when they bought it. Otherwise say what proof the maker asks for (usually the receipt).
- **No alarm, no hedging stack.** A recall that applies is stated plainly with what to do. One that doesn't is one line.
